01 & 02. Introduction & Understanding Mental Health

Being a child comes with many challenges and transitions. Just like physical health, mental health needs regular care and attention[cite: 11]. According to the WHO, health is a state of complete physical, mental, and social wellbeing[cite: 12]. Good mental health helps young people handle life changes, feel in control, and reach out for support when needed[cite: 12].

03. Risk and Protective Factors

Child Factors[cite: 15]

Risks: Genetic influence, chronic illness, low self-esteem, ineffective coping[cite: 15].
Protective: Secure attachment, effective emotional regulation, strong peer relationships[cite: 15].

Family Factors[cite: 15]

Risks: Family conflict, divorce, domestic violence, substance use[cite: 15].
Protective: Family harmony, positive parenting practices, parental emotional availability[cite: 15].

School & Community[cite: 13, 14]

Risks: Bullying, social isolation, negative teacher relationships[cite: 13, 14].
Protective: Anti-bullying programs, safe neighborhoods, inclusive school climate[cite: 13, 14].

04 & 05. Recognizing Signs of Concern Across Ages

Early Childhood (Infancy - 3 yrs)[cite: 23]

Significant sleep/eating difficulties, frequent intense tantrums, difficulty soothing themselves, and repetitive behaviors[cite: 23].

Childhood (3 - 9 yrs)[cite: 24]

Excessive general worries, unexplained drop in school performance, social withdrawal, sleep issues, and persistent sadness[cite: 24].

Adolescence (10 - 18 yrs)[cite: 25]

Persistent fatigue, suicidal ideation expressions, engagement in risky behaviors/self-harm, and drastic weight/sleep changes[cite: 25].

06. Common Mental Health Disorders

ADHD

Difficulties maintaining attention, impulse regulation, and restlessness in structured settings[cite: 27].

Anxiety & Mood Disorders

School refusal, excessive separation fears, anger outbursts, and persistent sadness[cite: 29, 33].

Learning Difficulties & Eating Disorders

Academic delays despite effort, homework avoidance, strict calorie tracking, and significant weight shifts[cite: 28, 30].

Bullying & Maltreatment

Repeated aggression by peers leading to school refusal or sudden withdrawal[cite: 32].

07, 08, 09, 10, 11 & 12. Talking, Professionals, & Self-Care

Communication Do's & Don'ts

Do: Build positive daily conversations, create safe environments, and listen actively without phone distractions[cite: 34].
Don't: View mental health as weakness, give dismissive advice, or compare with siblings[cite: 35].

Parent Self-Care

Manage your stress limits, set aside weekly time for personal hobbies/exercise, and stay socially connected with family and professionals[cite: 43, 44].

01. What Safeguarding Means

Safeguarding is the everyday work of protecting children's safety, welfare, and wellbeing. Child protection is the response when there is a concern that a child may be experiencing harm or neglect[cite: 52]. In both, the child's safety and best interests come first[cite: 52].

Recognising Forms of Harm[cite: 52]

  • Physical Harm: Hitting, shaking, or other physical injury[cite: 52].
  • Emotional Harm: Persistent threats, humiliation, or frightening treatment[cite: 52].
  • Sexual Abuse / Exploitation: Sexual requests or coercion, online or offline[cite: 52].
  • Neglect: Care or supervision needs that are not being met[cite: 52].

02. Notice Changes & Build a Safer Home

Notice patterns without accusing anyone. Make time for calm daily check-ins ("Was there anything today that made you feel worried?"), use non-violent discipline, and keep collection arrangements clear and updated.

03. If Your Child Tells You Something Worrying

Say This (DO)

  • "Thank you for telling me."
  • "You are not to blame."
  • "I will get help to keep you safe."

Avoid This (DON'T)

  • "Why didn't you tell me before?"
  • "You must have misunderstood."
  • Promising to keep secrets about safety.

04 & 05. Online Safety, Bullying, and Emergency Contacts

Review privacy settings together, talk regularly about online contacts, and preserve non-explicit evidence if concerns arise. For bullying, record details and report through school channels without posting accusations in parent groups.
